-------

Interpretation of these controls
--------------------------------
1. Masking slots after the nearest eight did not improve agreement relative to
   the hard-label PlayerV4 control (0.3965 vs 0.3990). Simple dilution from
   seeing 64 bullets is therefore not supported as the primary explanation.
2. A flat top-8 MLP trained with the same hard labels and split reached 0.4691,
   about 7 percentage points above hard-label PlayerV4. This is evidence that
   the attention pooling/optimisation path is a bottleneck, but it is not proof
   that the architecture lacks capacity: label objective and inductive bias
   differ in their distributional behaviour, and neither model generalised
   especially well.
3. Hard and low-temperature labels modestly improved argmax agreement over the
   original soft-T=1 objective (about 0.400 vs 0.387), while soft-T=1 had by far
   the best distribution match (KL 0.184 vs 0.411-0.471). This confirms an
   objective trade-off: minimising soft KL is not the same as reproducing the
   teacher's selected action.
4. The 0-bullet all-pad path is finite and highly learnable. The remaining
   weakness appears when bullet geometry matters, consistent with the earlier
   bullet-count diagnostics.

Decision
--------
PlayerV4 can represent and fit the teacher mapping on a fixed small dataset, so
Round 1 does not establish a fundamental model-capacity defect. However, the
flat top-8 MLP generalises materially better than the current single-query
attention encoder on the same held-out split, so that encoder remains a real
bottleneck candidate.

The immediate next experiment should not be DAgger or PPO. It should compare
attention and flat/ranked-top-8 models under matched parameter/update budgets,
with a hybrid hard+soft objective and more episode-level training diversity.
Closed-loop evaluation should happen only after held-out teacher agreement is
materially improved.
